Who Wins Creativity?
Brands, Ideas and the Age of AI.

14 April 2026

For decades, the advertising industry's biggest stages celebrated one thing above all else: the campaign.

The most memorable film. The smartest print ad. The cleverest headline. Creative excellence was often measured by what happened during a campaign window.

Today, that definition is changing.

Beyond the Campaign: Creativity Has Expanded

Awards such as Cannes Lions increasingly recognise work that extends far beyond advertising. Winning ideas are no longer confined to thirty seconds of storytelling or a striking visual. They're embedded in products, experiences, technology, culture and business itself.

The strongest brands don't simply communicate a message, they create value people can participate in.

Today's most celebrated work often lives across multiple dimensions:

  • Products that solve real problems.
  • Experiences that people remember.
  • Technology that enables new possibilities.
  • Culture that shapes conversations.
  • Business innovation that creates lasting impact.

In many ways, creativity has become less about interruption and more about integration.

This evolution reflects a broader shift in consumer expectations. Audiences no longer judge brands solely by what they say. They pay attention to what they build, how they behave and the role they play in culture. Creativity is increasingly measured by relevance, usefulness and lasting impact.

The AI Question: Tool or Threat?

Into this changing landscape arrives artificial intelligence.

Few innovations have sparked as much excitement or as much debate.

For some, AI represents the democratisation of creativity. It enables individuals and small teams to produce ideas, visuals and experiences that once required significant time and resources. It lowers barriers, accelerates execution and allows creative professionals to spend more time thinking than producing.

For others, AI raises uncomfortable questions. If anyone can generate beautiful imagery or compelling copy within seconds, what becomes truly valuable? Does creativity lie in execution or in the thinking behind it? Can originality exist when machines are trained on existing human work?

Perhaps the answer isn't found at either extreme.

History suggests that every technological leap has reshaped creative expression rather than replacing it. Photography didn't eliminate painting. Digital tools didn't end filmmaking. The internet didn't diminish storytelling. Instead, each innovation expanded the possibilities for those willing to explore it.

AI is likely to follow the same path.

What Will Creativity Reward Next?

The real differentiator will no longer be access to tools; it will be clarity of thought. The strongest ideas will continue to come from human insight, empathy and imagination. AI may accelerate the process, but it cannot define the purpose behind the work.

As the industry's definition of creativity continues to evolve, so too will the benchmarks for excellence.

The future won't belong to brands that simply create better advertising.

It will belong to those that create better reasons to be remembered.
